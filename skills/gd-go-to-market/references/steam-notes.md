# Steam notes — verify before acting

Last reviewed: 2026-10. Store rules change; check the Steamworks documentation (partner.steamgames.com/doc) and the event page for the specific festival edition before planning around any of these.

## Store and release
- **Steam Direct fee:** US$100 per app, recoupable once the game earns US$1,000 adjusted gross revenue.
- **Coming Soon page:** the store page must be public as "Coming Soon" for a minimum period before release (historically at least 2 weeks). Most indie developers keep it up for many months to collect wishlists.
- **Review process:** both the store page and the build are reviewed by Valve before going live; allow several business days for each.
- **Content survey:** developers declare mature content and AI-generated content (pre-generated and live-generated) on the content survey; the disclosure appears on the store page.
- **Wishlists:** wishlisters are notified at release and when the game is discounted by a meaningful amount.
- **Timing traps reported in 2026 guides (verify):** a first-time developer account waits a period after paying the fee before it can release; tax and identity verification can take weeks; the Coming Soon minimum counts only from the moment the page is actually posted as Coming Soon; reviews take several business days, so submit the page and the build at least a week before the date you need.
- **The build must contain what the page claims:** features, languages and controller support listed on the store page must be in the reviewed build.

## itch.io
- Keep a new page as a **draft** (or restricted) until it is ready: a game is shown in "most recent" lists only around its first public publish, and that moment cannot be repeated.
- Browser (HTML) builds are the fastest way to get strangers to play during Validate; label them as prototypes.

## Steam Next Fest
- Runs about three times a year (around February, June, October), one week each.
- **One participation per game, ever** — choose the edition carefully.
- The game must be unreleased through the end of the fest; a public store page (Coming Soon counts) is required to register; a playable demo must be public before the fest starts.
- Registration closes several weeks before the event (for October 2026 it closed on 31 August; for February 2027 it was announced as closing 10 January 2027, demo due 25 January — verify on Steamworks).
- Prepare: demo of the best 20–60 minutes, a livestream or pre-recorded broadcast, updated trailer, a store page that converts.

## Commonly cited indie heuristics (ESTIMATED, not rules)
- Wishlists at launch are the main predictor of first-week visibility; many indie marketing writers (e.g. Chris Zukowski, howtomarketagame.com) track thresholds in the thousands to tens of thousands for a healthy launch. Use comparable games' numbers from the market analysis instead of a universal number.
- First-week sales are often reported as a fraction of wishlists (frequently quoted ranges are around 10–20% over the first weeks), and vary widely by genre and price.
- Demos and festivals drive wishlist spikes mostly for games whose hook is clear at a glance.
